## 3. New findings

### N-1 — the controller gate asks a question the previous owner can answer however she likes {#n-1}

**Severity: Medium.** (Permanent destruction of another party's registry record. High impact, but
bounded: the registry holds no funds, and `archiveRock` releases the tag, so the recipient can
awaken the same physical rock under a fresh id. On a network where a rock id carries value, this is
a High.)

**Where:** `_requireRockController` L835-843 and `_accountAnswersTo` L853-861; `claimHandover`
L484-504, which changes `currentOwner` and deliberately leaves `smartAccount` alone.

**Narrative.** The hardening pass closed F-1 by replacing "the Safe is controlled by the owner"
with "ask the Safe". That is the right instinct, and against the *passive* case it works: after an
open gift the giver's Safe answers `isOwner(Bob) == false` and the gate refuses it. The two audit
PoCs and `testSmartAccountStopsBeingAControllerWhenItNoLongerAnswersToTheOwner` all pass, and they
are not passing for a trivial reason — the third uses a real mock Safe with a settable owner set.

But the question is put to a contract the giver still controls, and a Safe's owner set is writable
*by the Safe*. `addOwnerWithThreshold` and `removeOwner` carry Safe's `authorized` modifier, which
means "only the Safe itself" — which, for the 1-of-1 Safe a Rock Account is (`encodeSwapOwner` in
`web/src/lib/rock-account.ts` L517-523 confirms the shape: `SENTINEL -> owner -> SENTINEL`), means
"only Alice". One `execTransaction` over a MultiSend batch, signed by Alice alone:

```
addOwnerWithThreshold(BOB, 1)      // isOwner(BOB) is now true, so the registry admits this Safe
registry.archiveRock(rockId)        // Bob's rock is Archived, which is terminal by design
removeOwner(SENTINEL, BOB, 1)       // Bob is left holding nothing he did not have before
```

Bob never sees the intermediate state and gains nothing from it. She can equally `initiateHandover`
his rock to herself, or `markLost` it. The cost to her is one transaction.

The contract's own `@dev` at L823-829 now asserts the invariant as fact — *"Asking the account who
its owners are converts a sentence of prose into the invariant it always claimed to be."* It does
not: it converts one sentence of prose into another, because the answer is the attacker's to write.
This is precisely the structural observation the first audit closed on, one level down.

**Scope.** Only the open-gift path, and the named-gift path when the pre-signed owner swap fails to
land (`claim/route.ts` L134-143 reports `UNAVAILABLE` and the registry claim has already
succeeded). When the swap *does* land, the Safe is genuinely the recipient's and there is no
attack.

**PoC:** `test/audit/BankRockRegistryReview.t.sol::testReview_N1_theGiversSafeCannotBuyBackControlByAddingTheRecipientAsASigner`
— **fails today**, with a mock that carries Safe's real `authorized` guards so the batch is one a
single signing owner could genuinely submit.

**Recommended fix — the one the first audit already named.** Make the authority follow the object:
`claimHandover(uint256 rockId, address smartAccount, Attestation calldata att, bytes calldata sig)`
with `att.smartAccount == smartAccount`, writing `rock.smartAccount = smartAccount` beside
`rock.currentOwner`. D-029 already derives the Rock Account from *(tag, owner)*, so the recipient's
address is computable before the claim and the verifier can sign it — the machinery exists; the
signer sets the field to zero on the claim path only because nothing asked it not to
(`attestation.ts` L73-88 documents exactly this). It is an ABI change, which Part 2.2 explicitly
permits, and it closes N-1 and the open-gift consequence in [§4](#4-the-three-judgement-calls)
together.

If the ABI must not move, the cheaper variant: have `claimHandover` clear `rock.smartAccount`, and
add an owner-only `setRockAccount(uint256, address)` so the new owner binds their own. Either way
the registry stops recording an authority that D-029 says is stale the moment the owner changes.

### N-2 — `swapExactIn` accepts its own address as the recipient, and there is no rescue {#n-2}

**Severity: Low.** `XYCSwapTaker.sol` L197: `to` is passed through to the app with no check.
`to == address(this)` sends the output token into a contract whose documented policy is that
tokens sent to it are lost — and the taker's address is the one thing the user has just had in
their clipboard, because step 1 of every swap is approving it (`README.md` L167-169). A
one-line `if (recipient == address(this)) revert BadRecipient(recipient);` removes the whole class.
Worth doing precisely *because* the no-rescue decision (F-5) is the right one.

### N-3 — the Etherscan playbook omits the administrator functions and the attestation tuple format {#n-3}

**Severity: Informational.** See [§5](#5-etherscan-usability).

## 4. The three judgement calls

### F-19 — the plain-English renames were not done. I agree, and I would not revisit it.

The spec offers the fallback itself, and the fallback is executed properly: Etherscan renders the
first `@notice` sentence directly above the function, and the first four words of each are "Give
this rock away", "Collect a rock that was given to you", "Take back a gift you opened", "Retire
this rock permanently". A reader of the Write tab sees a sentence before they see
`initiateHandover`. The rename would buy a marginal improvement in the function *list*; it would
cost a synchronised change across the web chain library, the MCP ABI copy and a second agent's
signer, and every one of those is a place a typo becomes a silent mismatch.

One caveat, which is now live rather than hypothetical: if [N-1](#n-1) is fixed the way I recommend,
`claimHandover`'s signature changes and all three consumers move anyway. **That is the moment to do
the rename, and it is the only moment I would do it in.** Not before.

### F-21 — no `action` field in the attestation. I agree for Sepolia. I would require it before a value-bearing network.

The reasoning in the changes file is sound as far as it goes — the type string is a live contract
with a second agent's signer, and today's impact is genuinely nil, because `subject` is the same
person on both paths and `claimHandover` ignores `smartAccount` by design. I verified the signer
matches the six-field string byte for byte (`attestation.ts` L20-21 against L168-171 of the
contract), so the two implementations agree today.

But the argument that changing the type a third time is "the wrong trade inside a hardening pass"
is an argument about *this week*, not about the deployment. Three things make it worth taking
before anything valuable exists:

1. the type hash is a compile-time constant of an **undeployed** contract — it is free now and a
   migration with live rocks later;
2. the registry's own NatSpec (L54-56) describes the attestation as "a narrow bearer token", and
   the narrowness is currently enforced by which function the relayer happens to call, not by the
   signature;
3. the risk it protects against is the one the project is most likely to walk into — the verifier
   signing an attestation for a *non-claiming* purpose (a login proof, a check-in), at which point
   every such signature is silently also a valid `claimHandover` authorisation for that rock.

**Recommendation: add `bytes32 action` before the value-bearing deployment, and add it in the same
change as the [N-1](#n-1) fix,** which already breaks `claimHandover`'s ABI. One migration, not two.

### The open-gift consequence — a contract concern *and* a product one, and the contract half is the fixable half.

The state of affairs: after an open gift, `currentOwner` is Bob and `smartAccount` is still Alice's
Safe. Bob therefore has no sponsored path to any owner action — the account that could pay for him
is not his, and the registry (correctly, since the fix) refuses it. Bob must act from his own
wallet, with his own gas, which is the exact thing the rest of the system is built to avoid for a
person who has just tapped a rock for the first time.

It is tempting to file this as pure product, and the changes file implicitly does. I do not think
that survives contact with the second half of the same fact: **the Safe that is not Bob's is also
the Safe holding the rock's money.** D-027 states that for the Safe; nothing states it for the
registry, and the registry is what a reader consults to learn who controls a rock. So the open-gift
path hands over the record and not the treasury, and `describeRock` reports a `rockAccount` that
belongs to somebody else without a word of warning.

Three things follow, and they split cleanly:

- **Contract:** the registry should stop recording an account it knows to be stale. Binding the
  claimant's account during `claimHandover` ([N-1](#n-1)) fixes the authority bug, restores Bob's
  sponsored path, and makes `describeRock` truthful, all in one field. This is the fixable half and
  it is not expensive.
- **Product:** whether an open gift should exist at all in a flow where the money cannot follow it
  is a product decision, and a defensible answer is "yes, but say so" — the UI should tell the
  giver that an open gift moves the rock and not the account, and tell the recipient the same.
- **Interim, if neither lands before Sepolia:** the app should encourage named gifts, where the
  pre-signed owner swap does land and all of this evaporates.

Calling it "a product concern" is true of the *last* of those and not of the first. I would not sign
it off as purely product.

## 5. Etherscan usability

I read the NatSpec of every external function on both contracts. The Part 2 standard is met, and
met well — `describeRock` in particular does exactly what it was asked to do, and the "two things
never to do" section at `README.md` L31-43 is the best-judged part of the document.

**Can a person drive awaken → give → claim → retire from the Write tab with only the README?**

| Step | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **awaken** | **No, and correctly so.** `awakenRock` needs an attestation, which requires the tag's secret key. `README.md` L98-102 says this in plain words and does not pretend otherwise. This is the product working, not a documentation failure |

| **retire** | **Yes.** `archiveRock` L136-143, with "there is no undo" in bold, what survives as history, and the tag release explained — which is the single most important thing to understand before pressing it |

So: **the two actions Etherscan can drive, it can drive without a mistake. The two it cannot, it
explains.** That is the bar Part 2 set.

Three gaps were open at the first sign-off and **all three are now closed** (N-2, N-3):

1. **The administrator section exists.** `setAttester`, `pause`/`unpause` and the two-step
   ownership transfer each have a field table, and `renounceOwnership` is documented as a button

3. **The taker's `to` field** is documented as required, with both rejected values and the reason

One residual nit, unchanged: `verify.md` L57-59 still speaks of `Ownable.transferOwnership` where
the contract is `Ownable2Step`; the constructor-argument point it makes is still right.

One new one, introduced by the rebind: the `att` tuple table now tells an Etherscan reader that
`smartAccount` is "The Rock Account to bind. Not the zero address." It does not tell them that on a
**claim** this field replaces the rock's account and hands its holder the owner-action gate — which

## 8. Fourth look — the rebind-on-claim change

Scope: commits `5bf0d16`, `29f3b27`, `1c7bd00`, i.e. `git diff fc5ce97 -- contracts/contracts` plus
the claim route and the swap-recipient follow-ups. Read cold against the source, then against
`2026-09-12-changes.md` §3.

### What landed

`claimHandover` now writes `rock.smartAccount = att.smartAccount` (L534) and refuses a zero account
(L529); `HandoverClaimed` carries the bound account; the `Attestation.smartAccount` `@notice` is
rewritten to say the field is live on both attested paths. **The parameter list did not move** — the
field was already in the signed struct and was simply being ignored — so no consumer's call shape
broke and the three ABI copies plus `mcp/dist` are all current. That is a neat piece of work: it
gets the property the first sign-off asked for without the migration I had assumed it would cost.

Off chain, the claim route inverts its two halves — the pre-signed Safe owner swap is submitted and
its receipt awaited **before** `claimHandover` — refuses open gifts outright, and refuses an
attestation whose `smartAccount` differs from the one the registry currently holds. The taker now
rejects `to == address(0)` and `to == address(this)`; the "pay the caller" sentinel is gone.

`npm test`: **105 passing**, plus the one new PoC below. `solhint`: unchanged at 0 errors, 8
warnings. Slither: unchanged.

### N-1 is reduced, not closed {#n-1-fourth}

The registry never asks whether the account it is told to bind has anything to do with the new
owner. It takes `att.smartAccount` on trust and writes it.

That would be fine if the attester named the claimant's account. It does not.
`resolveSmartAccount` (`web/src/lib/nfc/rock-resolution.ts` L165-167) returns the rock's **existing**
account for every rock in `awake` or `handover_pending` — and a claim is by definition the latter —
so on the claim path the rebind writes back the address that was already there. The account becomes
the recipient's only because the route swapped the Safe's *owner* first. The authority follows the
object because a web route says so, not because the contract does.

So for any claim that does not go through that route — self-relaying a captured attestation, which
`contracts/README.md` explicitly invites; a future client-side broadcast; a hand-built Etherscan
call — the rock ends up owned by the recipient with the giver's Safe recorded as its account, and
the original N-1 batch works unchanged:

```
addOwnerWithThreshold(recipient, 1)   // the gate now admits this Safe
registry.archiveRock(rockId)          // the recipient's rock is destroyed, terminally
removeOwner(SENTINEL, recipient, 1)
```

**PoC:** `testReview_N1_aClaimNamingTheGiversAccountMustNotLeaveItInControl` — **fails**. It uses a
*named* gift and the exact attestation shape the real verifier produces, so it is not a contrived
input; the first round's PoC, which used an account the giver does not control, now passes.

**Fix.** One line, in `claimHandover`, beside the zero check:

```solidity
if (!_accountAnswersTo(att.smartAccount, att.subject)) revert NotRockOwner(rockId, att.smartAccount, att.subject, att.smartAccount);
```

(with an error of its own rather than that reuse). It makes the route's ordering an invariant for
every caller instead of a convention for one, and it costs one staticcall the contract already
knows how to make. **The caveat that must be weighed:** a Rock Account is counterfactual until its
first UserOp, and a counterfactual address has no code, so `_accountAnswersTo` is false for it. With
the route's ordering that is harmless — the owner-swap UserOp carries the `initCode` and deploys the
account before the claim — but a claim against a never-deployed account would revert until it
exists. If that is judged too sharp, the honest alternative is to stop claiming the invariant: say
in the `@dev` that the registry records the account the attester chooses and that the *attester* is
what stands between a recipient and the giver's Safe, and put the requirement on the signer instead.
What must not stand is the current text, which asserts a property the code does not enforce.

### N-6 — the swap is now load-bearing, and its success is not checked {#n-6}

**Severity: Medium (off-chain; belongs to the perimeter owner).**

The route's whole N-1 argument is "the Safe moves first, and if it does not land nothing is
claimed". Two holes in that:

1. **`success` is never read.** `submitSignedUserOp` (`web/src/lib/rock-account.server.ts`
   L435-441) types the bundler's reply as `{ receipt?: { transactionHash?: Hex }; success?: boolean }`
   and then returns `real({ txHash })` on the presence of a transaction hash alone. ERC-4337
   reports an *included but reverted* UserOperation with `success: false` and a perfectly good
   receipt. So a Safe owner swap that reverted — a stale `prevOwner` after any owner change, a
   nonce already used, a guard — is reported as landed, the route proceeds to `claimHandover`, and
   the recipient ends up owning a rock whose account is still the giver's. That is N-1, reached
   through the very check that was added to prevent it, and with a transaction hash to point at.
   Fix: `if (receipt.success !== true) return unavailable(...)`.
2. **No lifetime margin before the irreversible half.** `verifyAttestation`
   (`rock-account.server.ts` L124) rejects an attestation only once it has *expired*. A claimant who
   POSTs at deadline-minus-seconds passes every pre-check, the swap lands (up to 15 s of receipt
   polling), and the claim is then mined past `att.deadline` and reverts `AttestationExpired`. The
   giver is left owning the rock while the recipient owns the Safe **that holds the rock's money** —
   the mirror of N-1, reachable by one party's choice of when to press the button, and repeatable
   by tapping again. Fix: refuse when the remaining lifetime is under a margin (a minute or two
   covers the poll window), and re-check claimability immediately before broadcasting.

Both are one-liners, and the reordering is still the right call: its failure mode (giver keeps the
title, recipient keeps the account) is better than the old one, and the route reports the split
state honestly. It just has to be a real check.

### N-7 — the attester now chooses a rock's controller, and the threat model does not say so {#n-7}

**Severity: Low** (a property of a trusted key, not a bug — but an unlisted one).

Before this change the attester could already set `att.subject`, so it could move *title* on any
rock with a pending gift. It now also sets `att.smartAccount`, and the account it names becomes the
address `_requireRockController` will admit. Those are different powers, and the second is new:

- on a **named** gift the attester still cannot choose who receives the rock — `att.subject` must
  equal the recipient the owner named — but it can now choose **who controls it**;
- the account it names need not be a Safe. Any contract that answers `isOwner(newOwner) → true`
  passes `_accountAnswersTo`, so a compromised attester can install itself as a permanent
  co-controller of a rock it does not own, able to `archiveRock`, `initiateHandover` and `markLost`
  after the legitimate owner holds title;
- `describeRock().rockAccount` is what the app shows as "where this rock's money is". Rebinding does
  not move a single existing token, but it redirects the account of record, and therefore where the
  next deposit goes.

None of this is reachable without the attester key, and `claimHandover` is `whenNotPaused`, so the
pause switch does stop it. But the threat-model row in `specs/15-exit-demo-mode.md` Part 5 currently
describes the attester as authorising *claiming*, and that is no longer the whole of it. It should
say: **a compromised attester can move a rock's title within the constraints of an open gift, and
can bind that rock to an account of its own choosing.** This also raises, not lowers, the value of
spec 16 #17's separation of the attester key from the deployer key, and it is one more argument for
the `bytes32 action` field of [F-21](#4-the-three-judgement-calls).

**Can a claimant bind a rock to an account they do not control?** Not by choosing one — the field is
resolved server-side and the route re-checks it against the registry. The exposure runs the other
way: the claimant is bound to whatever the attester names, and today that is an account that is
*not yet* theirs and only becomes theirs if an off-chain step succeeds. What it costs them is
[N-1](#n-1-fourth) when that step is skipped and [N-6](#n-6) when it silently fails.

### N-8 — prose that describes code nobody wrote {#n-8}

**Severity: Informational**, and it is the same species as F-23 was.

`claimHandover`'s `@dev` (L500-509) says the attester "names the rock's *existing* account when the
claimant is about to become a signing owner of it … and the claimant's *own* account otherwise,
which is the open-gift path". `resolveSmartAccount` has no second branch — it returns the existing
account whenever the rock has one — and the app refuses open gifts outright, so the case the
sentence describes cannot arise and the behaviour it promises is not implemented anywhere. Either
implement the branch or describe the one rule that exists.

`2026-09-12-changes.md` §3 carries a stale warning alongside it: *"`attestation.ts` must set
`smartAccount` on claim attestations … Until it does, every claim reverts `InvalidSmartAccount`."*
It already does — `signTapAttestation` (`web/src/lib/nfc/verify.ts` L237-252) passes the resolver's
answer on every path, claim included. Claims work; the note should go before someone acts on it.
